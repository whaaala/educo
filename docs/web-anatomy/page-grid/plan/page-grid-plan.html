The page grid

One invisible set of columns runs across every page. Blocks land on it, snap to it and re-flow by it on every screen, so a photo in the hero and a card in the footer share the same edges without anyone measuring. You never see it unless you switch on Layout guides.

In plain words

A teacher drags a photo beside their words. Without being told, the photo takes exactly half the page and the words the other half. On a tablet it stays half and half. On a phone the photo goes under the words, full width. Three news cards further down line up with those same two halves, and so does the footer. Nobody typed a number.

That is the whole idea. The page is divided into 12 equal columns from tablet width up and 6 on a phone. The columns run from one edge of the page to the other: a block can be dragged right to the far left or far right. Between the columns there is no gap; the space between blocks comes from the blocks themselves, half each side (at least 0.75 rem between two blocks). The space at the sides of the page is a small default padding on each section (at least 0.8 rem), which you can drag or set to anything, down to zero. Paragraphs stop at a comfortable reading width (about 75 characters), so wide screens stay readable without shrinking the grid.

When a row cannot keep its shape on a narrower screen, it becomes the fewest equal columns whose words still fit: four cards go to two, then to one. Never a staircase, never a broken word.

shipped · G-1 → G-3c As built, two things read differently. The lines run gapless, edge to edge, and the space between two blocks is centred on a line, so three cards share one gap (G-3b). The page keeps a frame of 1 rem on a phone, growing to 1.25 rem wide, on all four edges; a coloured first or last section still reaches the edge, its words keep the frame inside (G-3c). next · G-3d today a block set to half the page keeps that half when it ends up alone on its phone line; it will take the whole line.

Free placement keeps the page whole (MUST) shipped · G-3b

Free placement is the person's choice, and the builder respects it. It must never make the page look broken. A freely placed block:

  • keeps its default margin and padding, so it never sits flush against a neighbour or the page edge unless the person sets that;
  • never overlaps its neighbours or pushes the page sideways: its partner gives up exactly what it takes (rule 19), and the row wraps instead of squeezing;
  • follows every screen by the same fit rule as snapped blocks: its share is kept where it fits, and the row becomes even columns where it does not;
  • keeps everything the person set (spacing, order, per-screen choices) when they switch back to snapping: the block moves to the nearest line, nothing else is lost, and Undo returns the free width.

The page check warns about anything that still looks wrong after a free move: words too close to an edge, a block overlapping another, or a row that leaves one block stranded.

changed · G-3b As built, free placement cannot leave the grid at all: an Alt-dropped block lands on the nearest lines, and the rest is a % margin inside its own columns, kept per screen. At every other screen it stays on its lines; where the fit rule steps a row, the free margin drops so the blocks stay equal. Back on the lines clears it in one Undo. Measured in the headed UAT (108 checks, six devices × four themes, Preview at all 70 screens): no overlap, no sideways scroll. next · G-5 the page check's "covers words" warning.

1 · Do blocks snap to the row step? you chose A

A · No, rows follow the content recommended

3 · Is the page grid set for the whole site, or page by page? you chose A shipped · G-1 · G-2

A · The whole site, a page may opt out recommended

One grid for every page, so the header, footer and content line up when a visitor moves between pages. A page that needs something different (a landing page on 16 columns) switches on "This page uses its own grid". Stored on the site, with an override on the page.
